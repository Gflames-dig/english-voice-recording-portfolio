## My Recording Workflow

### 1. Read and Understand the Complete Prompt

I review the full text and the specific recording instructions before recording. I check for names, numbers, abbreviations, punctuation, answer choices, pronunciation notes, and any special requirements for pace or tone.

### 2. Follow the Text Exactly

I read what the task requires, in the requested order, without paraphrasing, correcting, summarizing, or adding a personal response.

If the instruction asks me to read a question and every answer choice, I read all of them and do not select an answer unless the prompt explicitly asks me to.

### 3. Deliver the Text Naturally

I use clear articulation and a steady, conversational pace. I allow punctuation and meaning to guide pauses, and use emphasis where it improves comprehension.

I avoid both rushed delivery and an unnecessarily flat, robotic tone.

### 4. Record with Quality in Mind

I follow the project's setup guidance, use the designated recording tool, and aim for a quiet environment with consistent microphone distance and speaking level.

I listen for distracting background noise, clipping, distortion, excessive room echo, or changes in volume.

### 5. Review Before Submission

I compare the take against the written prompt, checking for omitted or substituted words, repeated phrases, unclear pronunciation, uneven pace, and recording defects.

If a take misses the requirements, I record it again rather than submitting a known problem.

### 6. Flag Uncertainty Instead of Guessing

If an instruction is contradictory, a name has no clear pronunciation guidance, or a prompt appears technically incomplete, I follow the project's escalation process and ask for clarification when appropriate.

I do not invent a pronunciation or silently change the text.

## Demonstration Samples

The written samples below demonstrate my planned read-aloud approach. They are original portfolio exercises, not representations of paid client assignments or published audio work.

### Sample 1: Clear, Natural Informational Read

**Script**

> Welcome. In this short guide, we will look at three practical ways to prepare for a productive day. First, choose the most important task and decide when you will begin it. Next, gather the information and tools you need before you start. Finally, take a moment to review your progress and adjust your plan if necessary.

**Delivery Approach**

- Use a warm, composed, informative tone.
- Keep the pace steady and the words easy to understand.
- Use brief pauses after the opening sentence and between the three steps.
- Give light emphasis to "First," "Next," and "Finally" to make the sequence clear.
- Preserve every word in the script; do not add an introduction or sign-off.

**Quality Checks**

- All three steps are included in the correct order.
- No words are substituted, repeated, or omitted.
- The pace remains consistent from beginning to end.
- The final sentence is complete and clearly articulated.

### Sample 2: Reading a Question and Every Answer Choice

**Prompt**

> Which item is used to measure temperature? A. Thermometer. B. Compass. C. Ruler. D. Stopwatch.

**Recording Instruction**

Read the question and every answer choice aloud. Do not choose or explain an answer.

**Delivery Approach**

Read the question clearly, then read A, B, C, and D in order. Leave a short, consistent pause between the choices.

Do not emphasize one option as if it were the selected answer, and do not add a response after the last option.

**Quality Checks**

- The question is read exactly as written.
- All four options are included in order.
- No option is skipped or paraphrased.
- No answer is selected and no explanation is added.

### Sample 3: Audiobook-Style Narrative Read

**Original Practice Passage**

> By the time the evening rain stopped, the road had turned quiet. Ada closed her notebook, listened to the water dripping from the roof, and looked again at the letter on the table. She had read it twice already, but one sentence still troubled her. Tomorrow, she decided, she would ask the question she had been avoiding.

**Delivery Approach**

Use a measured narrative pace, with a natural pause after "turned quiet" and after "on the table."

Keep the tone reflective, not melodramatic. Let the final sentence carry a little more intention while keeping the words intelligible and the volume controlled.

**Quality Checks**

- Maintain the passage's original wording and sequence.
- Use punctuation to guide phrasing without creating unnatural pauses.
- Keep character and narrative delivery consistent across the passage.
- Avoid overacting, swallowing word endings, or rushing the final sentence.

## Recording Quality Checklist

Before submitting, I check the following:

- **Text accuracy:** Every required word, number, and answer choice is present and in order.
- **Pronunciation:** Words are intelligible, and uncertain names or terms are handled according to project instructions.
- **Pacing:** The delivery is smooth, natural, and consistent, with pauses that support comprehension.
- **Voice consistency:** Volume, distance, articulation, and tone remain reasonably stable through the take.
- **Audio quality:** The recording is checked for background noise, echo, distortion, clipping, and abrupt cut-offs.
- **Instruction compliance:** No extra commentary, omitted text, paraphrasing, or unrequested interpretation has been added.
- **Final review:** The complete take is checked before it is submitted.

## Handling Common Recording Issues

**If I notice a word was changed or omitted:**  
I re-record the affected take in line with the project's submission rules and verify the corrected version against the prompt.

**If a name or term is unfamiliar:**  
I check any pronunciation guide supplied with the task. If the intended pronunciation remains unclear and accuracy matters, I flag the issue rather than make an unsupported guess.

**If the room or recording contains noise:**  
I assess whether the noise affects intelligibility or violates the stated quality standard. I improve the setup and re-record when needed instead of relying on processing to conceal a poor take.

**If feedback identifies uneven pacing:**  
I review the specific section, adjust pauses and phrasing while preserving the words, and submit a corrected take according to the project workflow.

**If written instructions conflict:**  
I do not resolve a material conflict by silently choosing one interpretation. I seek clarification or follow the designated escalation procedure.
